I study human behavior in the context of emerging technologies.

Welcome! I am Cagla (pronounced Chah-lah), a PhD Candidate at the Ross School of Business, University of Michigan, advised by Stephen Leider and Ruslan Momot.

My research uses behavioral experiments to understand how people make decisions when they interact with emerging technologies. It currently centers on two streams:

  • Privacy decision-making: studying how fatalism, the belief that a data breach is inevitable, affects privacy-related decisions.
  • Generative AI in teams: studying how collaborative environments influence the adoption and use of generative AI tools.

Across these projects, I design controlled experiments to uncover the behavioral mechanisms behind these decisions and draw managerial implications. Before Ross, I received my MSc and BSc in Industrial Engineering from Bilkent University.

Major Revision · Management Science

Privacy, Risk and Fatalism

with Stephen Leider and Ruslan Momot

Through a series of laboratory experiments, we study whether users act fatalistically, that is, believing a data breach is inevitable regardless of the actions they take, when making privacy-related decisions. We find that many users are fatalistic: they show little sensitivity to increases in data collection or breach risk, even when making decisions involving their real personal information.
